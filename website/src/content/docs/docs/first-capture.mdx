While Reqly captures, it's your computer's proxy: apps send their requests through it, and Reqly shows each one. When you stop, your network settings come back as they were.

## Start capturing

Do any of these:

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="Mac">

- Click **Start Capturing** in the toolbar.
- Choose **Capture › Start Capturing** (⌘R).
- Turn on the switch in Reqly's menu-bar item.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows">

- Click **Start capturing** in the toolbar, above the list of requests.
- Choose **Capture › Start capturing** (Ctrl+R).
- In the notification area, at the end of the taskbar, click Reqly's icon, and turn on the switch at the top of its panel. At first, the icon may be under **Show hidden icons**.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

While Reqly captures, the button reads **Capturing**, with a teal dot, and the status bar at the bottom says **Capturing on port 9090**.

## Allow the helper, once

On the Mac only: the first time you start capturing, Reqly asks you to allow its helper. The helper is a small program that changes your Mac's proxy settings while Reqly captures, and always puts them back.

1. In the **Allow Reqly to Set Your Proxy** sheet, click **Open System Settings**.
2. In **General › Login Items & Extensions**, turn on **Reqly** under **Allow in the Background**.

Capturing starts as soon as you allow it.

On Windows, there's no helper to allow. Reqly changes your proxy settings itself, without administrator rights, and capturing starts at once.

:::note[Your settings always come back]
<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="Mac">

The helper changes only the HTTP and HTTPS proxy of your Mac's network services. It puts them back when you stop capturing, when you quit Reqly, and even if Reqly quits unexpectedly. If your Mac shuts down while Reqly captures, the helper puts them back when the Mac starts up again.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows">

Reqly changes only the HTTP and HTTPS proxy in your Windows account's proxy settings. It puts them back when you stop capturing, when you quit Reqly, and even if Reqly quits unexpectedly. If your PC shuts down while Reqly captures, Reqly puts them back the next time it opens.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>
:::

## Watch requests come in

Open an app or a web page, and its requests appear in the list, each with the app that sent it. HTTPS requests show as **Encrypted connection** until you decrypt their host. See [Decrypt HTTPS](/docs/https/).

Select a request to see its headers, body and timing. See [The request list and details](/docs/requests/).

## Stop capturing

Click **Capturing**, or press the shortcut again: ⌘R on the Mac, Ctrl+R on Windows. Reqly puts your proxy settings back.

The requests stay in the list until you clear them or quit Reqly. To clear them, choose **Capture › Clear Traffic** (⌘K) on the Mac, or **Capture › Clear traffic** (Ctrl+K) on Windows. Quitting deletes them, unless you [save them as a session](/docs/sessions/).

On Windows, closing Reqly's window doesn't quit it: while **Show Reqly in the notification area** is on in **Settings › General**, Reqly keeps running there, and keeps capturing if it was. To quit, choose **File › Quit Reqly** (Ctrl+Q), or click Reqly's icon in the notification area and choose **Quit Reqly**.

## Start capturing when Reqly opens

Open Settings, with **Reqly › Settings…** on the Mac, or the gear button at the right end of the title bar on Windows. In **General**, turn on **Start capturing when Reqly opens**.

## Apps that don't use your proxy settings

Most apps use your computer's proxy settings, but some command-line tools and apps with networking of their own don't. Point them at Reqly yourself, at `127.0.0.1` and Reqly's port. For example:

<Tabs syncKey="os">
<TabItem label="Mac">

```bash
curl -x 127.0.0.1:9090 http://example.com
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Windows">

```powershell
curl.exe -x 127.0.0.1:9090 http://example.com
```

In PowerShell, type `curl.exe`: there, `curl` alone can stand for PowerShell's own `Invoke-WebRequest`.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

For an app that can't use a proxy at all, use a [reverse proxy](/docs/connections/#reverse-proxies).
